What predicts football ability: psychology, technique, or physique?

A data pipeline from raw player data → Supabase → SAS Viya modelling → this page.

1. The question

Coaches argue about what makes a great footballer: the mind (composure, vision, decision speed), the craft (passing, dribbling, shooting), or the body (pace, strength, stamina). This project measures all three. We model a player's overall ability from 29 rated attributes split into three groups — psychological, technical and physical — and ask how much each group really explains, and which individual attributes carry weight.

2. The data

The base is the FIFA 22 complete player dataset (~19,000 players). Each player has an overall ability rating (0–99) and 29 attributes we group as:

Psychological (7)

composure, aggression, vision, positioning, penalties, interceptions, reactions

Technical (15)

crossing, finishing, heading, short/long passing, volleys, dribbling, curve, FK accuracy, ball control, shot power, long shots, marking, standing & sliding tackle

Physical (7)

acceleration, sprint speed, agility, balance, jumping, stamina, strength

Honest caveat: these are scout-rated video-game attributes, not clinical measurements. The "psychological" group is a proxy for a psychological profile, not a validated personality instrument — read results as "how well do rated attributes track ability", and note that FIFA's reactions and composure partly act as summary ratings, which inflates the psychological group's apparent power.

3. What the research literature says

  • A meta-analysis of 4,101 athletes found conscientiousness and extraversion positively correlated with performance (PMC10813302).
  • A study of women's football showed psychological factors add predictive power beyond physical ability (PMC10090955).

The theme: psychological traits are real but partial predictors — which is exactly what we test here, now against technique and physique.

4. A crash course in the models & metrics

OLS regression
Ordinary Least Squares draws the best straight-line relationship: each attribute contributes a fixed amount and they're summed. Errors are made as small as possible.
R²
Share of ability variation explained, 0–1. We fit each group alone (standalone R²) and all together (full R²) to see what each adds.
Standardized coefficient (β)
Each attribute on a common scale so bars are comparable. Bigger = stronger independent contribution.
Why some bars are negative or tiny
Multicollinearity. Many attributes overlap (a good dribbler is usually a good ball-controller). When predictors carry the same information, the model splits credit between them, and some flip negative or become statistically insignificant — meaning they add nothing once the others are present, not that they're irrelevant on their own.
Decision tree
Instead of one line, it splits players with yes/no questions and predicts the group average — capturing nonlinear patterns a line misses. Its R² here is in-sample and unpruned, so treat it as an upper bound, not a fair head-to-head. (A true random forest isn't licensed on this Viya, so we use a single tree, PROC HPSPLIT.)

5. The pipeline

  1. Ingest — raw CSV cleaned in Python, loaded into Supabase Postgres.
  2. Model — SAS Viya reads the table over the REST API and fits group-only models, a full 29-variable OLS, and a decision tree, with overall as the target.
  3. Persist — fit statistics, per-variable coefficients, per-position fit and predictions are POSTed back to Supabase.
  4. Visualize — this page reads those tables live and renders everything below.

7. What it tells us

Interpretation will populate once results load…

The headline is counter-intuitive: in this data the psychological group alone explains far more of ability than technique or physique, and adding the latter two lifts R² only modestly. A big part of that is an artefact — FIFA's reactions and composure ratings double as overall-quality summaries. The honest reading: rated mental attributes track ability extremely closely in this dataset, technique adds a meaningful but smaller slice, and raw physique explains the least on its own.
